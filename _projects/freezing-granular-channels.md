nature_caption: "In high-altitude farmland, freeze-thaw cycles shape nutrient transport and soil structure. In thawing permafrost, shifting microstructure and spreading cracks may help determine ground stability and subsequent greenhouse gas release. In biology, cryopreservation works well for individual cells, but similar techniques fail for whole organs like kidneys and livers, which are dense, porous networks of cells and vesicles.\ The common thread is the freezing front within a network of pores saturated with water. While flow of gas or oil through saturated pores are being studied for long, there is a clear gap when it comes to a solid-liquid front. How it advances through a confined, interconnected medium depends on geometry, granular contacts, diffusion kinetics, and an ever-changing temperature gradient, all across multiple scales. Mapping how these factors combine is the key to controlling freezing, and it’s the question we’re after."
